The short version

The single most important finding: there is no 7 to 9 bedroom luxury home with true ski-in/ski-out to Snowbird. The big canyon homes are all at the Alta end (walk to Alta lifts, shuttle to Snowbird), and the Snowbird-side ski-in/out inventory is townhome clusters on Blackjack Road. Vail has more of what you asked for, and the standout is a gated, ski-in/ski-out complex at Golden Peak where two or three large units sit in the same building, next door to the kids' ski school.

Top 3 overall

1. Northwoods combo, Vail ($62,400). It is the only option that clears every one of your hard requirements at once: true ski-in/out (the gate opens onto the Riva Bahn base), 11 real bedrooms so every couple gets a door and Mom and the nanny get a two-bed room, a bunk room for the kids, a 14 person dining table in the larger unit, and a heated pool, hot tub, steam room and sauna on site. It is two condos rather than one chalet, but they are in the same gated building and the kids' ski school is next door, which is exactly what makes an 8 night trip with four toddlers work. Vail Village dining is a 5 to 7 minute walk.

2. Village at Sugarplum #6 + #15, Snowbird ($37,225). The best Snowbird can do for this group, and it is a good answer: two townhomes in the same small complex on the Peruvian side, ski to WhoDunnit from the end of the road, return via Peruvian Express or the Tram. Each unit has an indoor hot tub. The trade-offs are honest ones: #15 is only 1,100 square feet, garages fit nothing bigger than a mid-size SUV, and there is no walkable village, so evenings are at home or a shuttle ride to the Cliff Lodge.

3. 184 W Meadow Drive, Vail ($64,474). If you want the one-incredible-house version, this is it: a new European-style chalet on Gore Creek across from the Four Seasons with 7 ensuite bedrooms, three steam showers, a dining table for 14, a creekside hot tub and heated patios. It is not ski-in/out (about an 8 to 10 minute walk to Gondola One), but the listing includes ski storage next to Gondola One, which removes the daily gear haul. Bedroom fit is the compromise: 3 kings, two rooms with two doubles, one double-plus-twin, and a 6 twin bunk room, so one couple or Mom and the nanny end up in a double-bed room.

Dates

I checked Jan 5 to 13, Jan 6 to 14 and Jan 7 to 15 on every finalist that showed a live calendar. Inventory and pricing were nearly identical across the three, so the choice comes down to travel days. All three keep you on the mountain for Erin's birthday on Sunday, January 10.

Recommended. 8 nights, 7 ski days (Fri through Thu). Both weekend days are mid-trip for the birthday. You leave Friday morning before MLK weekend arrivals clog I-70 westbound and Little Cottonwood inbound. Every finalist below is available on these dates unless noted.
Wed Jan 6 to Thu Jan 14
8 nights, 7 ski days. Slightly cheaper at the Nicholson Chalet ($55,311 vs $56,570). Sugarplum #04 and Powder Ridge 12B are still blocked, so the Snowbird combo does not improve.

8 nights, 7 ski days. Midweek flights are cheapest. 184 W Meadow is $3,085 more on these dates; Northwoods and the Gore Creek house are the same price. Avoids any MLK overlap entirely.

Snowbird vs Vail for this trip

Snowbird

January snow Wins
Avg 89" in January on a 73" base vs Vail's 53" on 44". Roughly 1.7x the snow. Steeps stay open.
Expert terrain Wins
Sustained steeps off the Tram, Peruvian and Mineral Basin. Chutes, cliffs, real fall line.
Snowboard terrain
Boarders are welcome everywhere at Snowbird, but the classic Alta-Bird day is off the table (Alta is skiers only) and Snowbird's best zones involve long traverses and 3.5 mile runouts.
Ski-in/out inventory
Thin. Townhome clusters on Blackjack Road, or the Cliff Lodge. No big luxury homes.
Dining and apres
Self-contained resort: Tram Club, Aerie, SeventyOne, Steak Pit, El Chanate. Fine for a few nights, thin for eight.
Kids and childcare
Superior Child Care at the Cliff Lodge, 2 months to 12 years, $205 full day (2026-27 posted). Level 3 family pool. Very little else for non-skiing kids.
Airport Wins
SLC is 35 to 45 minutes away in good conditions and is a major hub for rebooking.
Closure risk
Real. SR-210 crosses 64 avalanche paths; several road events per winter, interlodge confinement possible. Staying in the canyon converts closures into private ski days, but arrivals and departures can slip.
Non-ski time
Cliff Spa (16+), scenic tram, snowshoe tours. Not much for the nanny and toddlers beyond the pool.

Vail

January snow
Reliable front side; Back Bowls and Blue Sky Basin can be thin or closed in a low-snow early January.
Expert terrain
5,317 acres, 42% advanced. Seven Back Bowls plus Blue Sky Basin. Huge variety, less sustained steepness than Snowbird.
Snowboard terrain Wins
No restrictions, three terrain parks, and the whole mountain is open to everyone. Flat bowl runouts are the one gripe.
Ski-in/out inventory Wins
Northwoods, Arrabelle, Lodge at Vail chalets, Forest Road and Beaver Dam homes. Plus walkable villages for everything else.
Dining and apres Wins
75+ restaurants across two pedestrian villages, Sweet Basil, Matsuhisa, Pepi's, Red Lion live music. Eight nights of variety and a real birthday dinner.
Kids and childcare Wins
Golden Peak and Lionshead children's ski schools, Small World nursery at Golden Peak, Adventure Ridge tubing, two ice rinks, Imagination Station. The nanny has options every day.
Airport
DEN is 2 to 2.5 hours over two passes with I-70 closure risk; EGE is 35 minutes but a small, weather-prone airport with limited flights for 16 people.
Closure risk
I-70 closures are common but usually hours, not days, and you are never confined indoors.
Non-ski time Wins
Shopping on heated walkways, spas, skating, snowmobiling, Nordic on the golf course, tubing.

My call: Vail, for this specific trip. If this were 12 advanced skiers with no kids, Snowbird would win on snow and terrain and I would send you to the Sugarplum townhomes. But this is Erin's birthday week with four toddlers, two snowboarders who cannot ride Alta, a grandmother and a nanny who need something to do, and eight nights of dinners for 16. Vail is the only one of the two that offers true ski-in/out lodging with 11 real bedrooms, a kids' ski school next door, and a walkable village for the birthday dinner. The trade you are making is snow depth and steepness for everything else, and in early January on a pass-holder week that is a trade I would make. Buy the Epic Pass or Epic 7-Day now (prices rise Sept 7 and sales end early December); Snowbird is on Ikon if you go the other way.

If snow is the deciding factor for the group, the honest Snowbird pick is Sugarplum #6 + #15 at $37,225, and you should hold the Northwoods units in Vail as the fallback until the Sugarplum contracts are signed.
